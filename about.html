About

How I got from a Commodore 64 to building AI platforms.

TL;DR

  • Started with a Commodore 64 and an Amiga, built my own PCs and broke a few Linux installs.
  • Studied Economics, Business Administration and Computer Science in the Netherlands (Maastricht) and Sweden (Jönköping). Learned Python along the way.
  • Worked on BI and MLOps at Generali, including a stint in Milan, then built the MLOps team at Covestro.
  • After Covestro, I founded byteCare and worked as a consultant at qdive (now Wavestone) and Comma Soft.
  • Today I work on AI engineering and AI infrastructure at byteCare.

The early days

My first computers were a Commodore 64 and an Amiga in the 1980s. I remember swapping floppy disks, trying to get RAM expansions to work and drawing pixel art in Paintbrush on Windows 3.11.

At some point using computers wasn’t enough. I wanted to know how they worked, so I built my own PCs and fought with Windows ME and 2000. I saved up for a 3DFX Voodoo graphics card, so games finally looked good.

My first programming happened in config files. I tweaked autoexec.cfg and config.cfg to get a few more frames per second out of Counter-Strike. That got me into competitive gaming in the ESL: an Elsa 56k modem, heavy CRT monitors carried to LAN parties and matches organized on IRC, long before Discord.

I also tried Linux early on (SuSE, later Ubuntu) and, of course, accidentally wiped my entire hard drive 🤷.

University

I didn’t want to study computer science in isolation, so I chose a degree combining Economics, Business Administration and Computer Science. I studied in the Netherlands (Maastricht) and Sweden (Jönköping), which came with a few memorable tech moments:

  • iPhone: My first smartphone was an iPhone 3GS. Having maps and mobile internet in my pocket was a big deal.
  • Spotify: It launched while I was in Sweden. I wanted it so badly that I asked a fellow student I had just met to sign me up with his credit card, because I didn’t own one yet.
  • Mac: On my flight to Sweden, the airline destroyed my Windows laptop. So I bought a Mac. Haven’t switched back since.

This is also when I learned Python, my first real programming language. I still use and love it today.

Career

Generali

I started my career at Generali as a technical project manager in Business Intelligence. I soon moved closer to the infrastructure and helped introduce Hadoop as part of Generali’s data architecture. BI taught me that whatever you build, a big part of the work is cleaning and shaping data.

In Milan (Italy), I led the implementation of Generali’s first ML-powered business process. Besides the tech, that meant a lot of project management and getting the people involved on board. Training the model was the easy part. Running it reliably in production was hard, so I moved into MLOps and worked a lot with Kubernetes and OpenShift, before MLOps was a common term.

Covestro

At Covestro, I built the MLOps team and platform from scratch, running entirely on AWS with CI/CD pipelines and infrastructure as code. Besides Python, I started using Terraform, and Go or TypeScript when a project needed it.

Consulting and byteCare

After Covestro, I founded byteCare and moved into consulting, first as Lead AI Engineer at qdive (now part of Wavestone) and then as Principal Consultant for MLOps at Comma Soft, helping clients build and run their ML and AI platforms.

Today I work on byteCare full-time. My focus is AI engineering and AI infrastructure: platforms that run LLM-based applications and assistants in production. I help organizations use AI that is cost-effective, performant and privacy-preserving, without giving up control over their data or blowing their infrastructure budget.

What drives me

I like taking things apart to see how they work, and then building something useful with them. That was true for the Amiga and it’s true for AI platforms. The tools change every few years. That part hasn’t.
